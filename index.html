Wolves: wild animals of the forest

Wolves live in families in the wild. This page tells you how they hunt, how they live, and why people think different things about them.

Basic facts

The gray wolf is the biggest wild dog. Most adult wolves are 30 to 80 kilograms. Wolves are not all the same size. Arctic wolves are big. Wolves in the Middle East are smaller.

Life in a pack

A wolf pack is a family, not strangers. Mom, dad, and their young wolves live together. They hunt together and take care of their home.

Parents

Mom and dad lead the pack. Usually, only they have babies.

Young wolves

Wolves born last year. They help hunt and take care of the babies.

Pups

Baby wolves. They are born in spring. At first, they cannot see. The pack takes care of them.

Home area

A pack has its own land. It can be from 130 to 2,600 square kilometers big.

How they hunt

Wolves are not very fast, but they can run for a long time. They run at 8 km/h for many hours. When they are near an animal, they can run up to 60 km/h. One wolf cannot catch a big animal alone. So the pack hunts together. They look at a group of animals and choose the weakest one.

Many hunts do not work. A big animal can kick a wolf and hurt it. So the pack checks many animals first. Then they choose one they can catch.

Wolves need our help

In the past, people hunted wolves a lot. Wolves also lost their homes in nature. By the 1950s, there were almost no wolves in Western Europe and most of the USA. Since the 1970s, people help wolves come back. In 1995, people brought wolves back to Yellowstone National Park. Today, some wolves are doing better. But some are still in danger.
